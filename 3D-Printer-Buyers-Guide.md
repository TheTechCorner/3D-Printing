## So you want to purchase that 1st 3D printer

Before diving in, here are a few things worth knowing about the 3D printing world:

- **Metric is the standard.** Printer specs, model dimensions, filament diameters, and nozzle sizes are all measured in millimeters (mm). Temperatures are in Celsius.
- **Patience is required.** Most prints take hours — sometimes tens of hours. This is normal.
- **Failed prints happen.** Expect a learning curve. Bed adhesion, warping, and stringing are common early hurdles.
- **Supports are often needed.** Overhangs steeper than ~45° typically require temporary support structures that you remove afterward.
- **Humidity is the enemy.** Filament (especially PLA, PETG, and TPU) absorbs moisture from the air and prints poorly if left out. Airtight storage with desiccant helps.
- **You'll need basic tools.** A scraper, flush cutters, and calipers go a long way.
- **The community is enormous.** There are countless forums, subreddits, and YouTube channels full of people happy to help troubleshoot.

Here is a list of items to consider when buying a 3D printer.

- Purpose and Use: What are your uses for a 3D printer. Are you a Hobbyist, Artist, Student, Teacher. Maybe a professional that will be doing prototyping

- Type of Printer:
  - **FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling):** Melts a plastic filament and deposits it layer by layer through a heated nozzle to build up the object. Think of it like a hot glue gun on a robot arm.
  - **SLA (Stereolithography):** Uses a laser or UV light to cure liquid resin into solid layers, one slice at a time. Known for high detail and smooth surfaces.
  - **SLS (Selective Laser Sintering):** A laser fuses powdered material (usually nylon) layer by layer. Great for complex geometries and functional parts without supports.

- Print Materials: What print materials do you want to use? There is PLA, ABS, PETG, TPU, Nylon, ASA and resin.

- Build Volume: What size of prints do you plan on doing?

- Ease of Use: Consider features like touchscreen interfaces, heated print beds, auto-leveling, and filament sensors, can make the printer more user-friendly, especially for beginners.

- Software Compatibility: Research which slicing software the printer supports. Popular options include Cura, PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio, and OrcaSlicer.

- Community and Support: Investigate the user community and manufacturer support. Active forums and responsive help are invaluable when you're troubleshooting.

- Cost: Look beyond the sticker price. Factor in ongoing filament costs, replacement parts, maintenance, and electricity.

- Safety Features: Safety features to look for, such as enclosed build chambers, fume filters, and thermal runaway protection, particularly important in educational environments or homes with children.

- Upgradability - Reparability: Can the printer be upgraded with new components or firmware to extend its lifespan and improve functionality. How easy is it to repair or how difficult is it to get repair parts?

- Multiple Colors: Would you like to be able to print multiple colors automatically?
